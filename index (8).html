HOW IT WORKS

How a report gets on the site

Simple: someone sends a report, the site checks that it is usable, and it can appear as Unreviewed. MOAT checks stronger claims later.

01

Someone reports it

We ask for where it was seen, when it was seen and what happened. A photo, source link or bus number helps, but not every field is compulsory.

02

The site does a basic check

It blocks obvious spam, empty reports and exact duplicates. This is only a quick check. It does not prove the report is true.

03

MOAT reviews it later

We can correct or remove weak entries. If good evidence exists, the label can change to Corroborated or Verified.

UnreviewedA public submission that MOAT has not checked yet.
VerifiedClear reporting, photos or video confirm the bus was used in a detention-related incident.
CorroboratedThere is good supporting evidence, but one detail is still unclear.
ContextUseful related information, but not enough to call it a detention-bus sighting.

We do not guess

If a bus number, operator or exact detail is unclear, the site says so instead of filling in the gap.

No live tracking

The map shows approximate, retrospective locations. It does not show live routes, exact movements or private details about protesters.

ABOUT

Why document detention buses?

Buses are usually understood as ordinary transport infrastructure. This archive documents the moments when buses are instead reported or documented as part of protest detention operations, and keeps the evidence, uncertainty and context visible.

What MOAT is recording

MOAT Detention Bus Archive is a civic archive by Ministry of All Transport Ever. Its primary subject is buses used to detain or transport protesters across India, with Delhi as the most detailed current collection and other cities and states added as evidence is found or submitted.

The archive is built around records, not rhetoric: a date, an approximate place, a description, a source and an evidence status.

What it is not

  • Not a live police tracker.
  • Not a database of individual protesters.
  • Not a claim that every police bus is removed from scheduled passenger service.
  • Not a substitute for original reporting or legal records.

Why include source links?

So every public claim can be checked against the reporting or footage it came from.

Can an entry be corrected?

Yes. Open a record and use “Suggest a correction”. The report form opens with that record already referenced, so your correction is submitted against the right entry. It does not silently edit the public record.

Why no exact coordinates?

The public-interest value is retrospective documentation, not real-time operational tracking.

REPORT A BUS

Report a detention-bus sighting

Submit what you observed or found. Be precise about what is visible and leave fields blank when you do not know. A qualifying report may appear immediately as an Unreviewed Community Report.

Good submissions are boringly specific.

Useful details:

  • date or approximate date
  • neighbourhood or landmark
  • bus/operator marking if visible
  • original post, video or article
  • what the bus was doing

Do not include private personal details about ordinary protesters.

A public article, post or video helps verification.
